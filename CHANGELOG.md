# Changelog

Everything that changes for someone using Hollowfall goes here, newest first, in the [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) shape. Versions follow [semver](https://semver.org/); while the major version is 0, a breaking change bumps the minor. Anything that changes the world generated for a given seed counts as a breaking change.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- A character that lasts across runs (`Hollowfall.Core.Progression`, issue #3, phase 2 of `docs/relations.md`). You earn experience for the Husks, Spitters and Wardens you defeat (creatures give none), reach levels up to 20, and get a stat point for each level after the first. The two stats, Vigour and Might, are saved in `progression.json` in the data folder (`HOLLOWFALL_PROGRESSION` moves it). The simulation does not read the stats yet, and there is no screen to spend points.

- Adaptive ambient music (`Hollowfall.Core.Audio.Music`, described in [docs/music.md](docs/music.md)), Scottish-flavoured and entirely synthesised in code (no audio files): modes (Dorian, Aeolian, Phrygian, major pentatonic), a drone on the root and fifth, a plucked clarsach (Karplus-Strong), a bodhran-like frame drum, a breathy whistle, warm detuned-saw pads, a brass and choir swell for the boss, a high glassy shimmer and a heartbeat, in a 8-bar 4/4 loop at 84 BPM. The key and tunes come from the world seed; the surface is Dorian, floors follow the dungeon themes (cold Aeolian, slower mossy harp, ashen Phrygian with a heavier drum) and the Wulver's hill has a warm major-pentatonic whistle tune. Every layer is a mono loop of exactly the same length, so they stay in step and cross over without clicks. `MusicMixer` turns a `MusicState` (floor, encounter, enemies near, style rank, health, boss, death, the Wulver, a hearth, dialogue, pause, a passive creature, linked portals) into smoothed layer gains: combat comes in fast and leaves slowly with a hold so it does not flicker, the heavy drum has hysteresis, dialogue ducks the music to 40 percent, pausing ducks it and removes the highs, and dying fades everything out over about 3 seconds. Short stingers (floor complete, boss intro, death, ending, the Wulver's greeting motif) play over it. `MusicDirector` renders the loops on a background task at startup and plays them as one `AudioStreamSynchronized` per context. New **Music volume** setting (0 to 100 percent, default 60; `GameSettings.MusicVolume`). The Ambience slider now scales the music's drone layer once the music is ready; the old looping drone stays only as a fallback, so the two never double.
- `MusicState.NearHearth` and a `Hearth` layer (a low hum and a soft harp) for the fire pits and hearths another feature is adding; nothing sets it yet.
- Fire pits and something warm to find. Fire pits (a ring of rough stones, crossed logs and a flame of glowing gems that flickers and sways) burn in some Safe, Standard and Treasure rooms: never in entrance, boss or trap rooms, never beside a door, stairs, chest, key or note, always on open floor with a free ring around them, and every dungeon has at least one on its first two floors (`FirePitPlacer`, a pure function of the seed). They share the few real lights with the wall torches, crackle softly as you near them, and harm nobody. One of them holds a surprise, found by following the warmth; what it says is in Irish, and `docs/gaeilge.md` records where every word comes from and asks a fluent speaker to check it before release. Behind it: a new low-poly `fire_pit` model (about 270 triangles) and one more small model (about 390, under its own budget, the catalogue still under 3000), three new synthesised sounds appended to `SoundId` (a soft crackle and two others, two of them seamless loops), a small once-per-run warmth that never amounts to a big boost, and what it remembers saved in the story progress file. Screenshot helpers: new `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO` values and showcase pages (see `docs/art-style.md`).

- Gàidhlig (Scottish Gaelic) and a Language setting. Settings has a Language chooser at the top, naming each language in itself (English, Gàidhlig, Gàidhlig agus Beurla), and changing it rebuilds every screen at once; it is saved with the other settings (`GameSettings.Language`, English by default, anything unknown in a saved file is English) and Reset keeps it. Bilingual shows the Gàidhlig with the English in brackets, or beneath it for longer text. The words live in `Hollowfall.Core.Localization` (`Loc`, a per-screen service with no global state, and string tables with a review status and a cited source for every Gàidhlig string; a key with no Gàidhlig shows its English and a missing key is recorded in `Loc.MissingKeys`). The title, pause, journal, controls and settings screens, the HUD, hints, prompts and banners all take their text from keys.
- What is in Gàidhlig is only what could be checked against a source: menu words (Cluich, Roghainnean, Fàg an-seo, Lean air, Leabhar-latha, Sìol, Cànan), a few actions and HUD labels, two floor-card names (An Sgòrnan, An Doras Deireannach), a line shown with a death (Feuch a-rithist), the Gàidhlig names of the creatures docs/mythology.md describes, and a Gàidhlig name for every dungeon under the title (`GaelicNames`: a place-name noun, then an adjective after it, lenited after a feminine noun, such as Cnoc Dubh or Beinn Mhòr). The seed words (`amber-hollow-914`) and the English dungeon names are unchanged. Eòghann greets in Gàidhlig, with the English beneath, in Gàidhlig and Bilingual modes. The long story text stays English. Nothing has been reviewed by a native speaker yet: see docs/gaidhlig.md, which lists every string with its status and source, and what needs a speaker.
- Settings now scrolls, so a longer language never pushes its buttons off a small screen, and the Controls screen has a wider action column.
- Mixed room links (`LinkKind`, `LinkMix`). Rooms are joined by walkable corridors, secret doors and **gaps**: every room is guaranteed a portal gap (two hallways, each with a door cell at the room, a landing, three steps and a lip, facing across a 6 to 16 cell pit of bare stone, spikes or water that cannot be crossed on foot) and a few deeper links are shaft gaps (a tunnel high above a sealed block). The first link out of the entrance room is a short bare gap. Room graph, depths and roles are unchanged; gaps are cut into a straight stretch of the link's corridor, so a floor that cannot give every room one is dug again with the same rooms, and about one floor in ten has to be re-split (its rooms differ from before). New `BlockType.Spikes`, `Tile.Spikes` and `CellKind` pit values, all appended at the end of their enums.
- `GapCrossing` finds a way over each gap with the real `PortalSystem.Place` and `PortalWalker`, and `DungeonReach` proves every room, the exit stairs and the boss are reached by walking plus validated hops. Falling into a pit costs 8 health on stone, 20 on spikes, none in water (a longer climb), never the last point, then the sim puts the player back at the last safe cell on their side (`PlayerFell`, `PlayerRecovered`). Enemies stop at the lip. The first time the player stands at a lip, a caption names their real portal bindings (`GapHints`, `PitEdgeReached`).
- Torches at every pit edge and in long hallways, minimap markers for hallways and pits, and line of sight across a gap reveals the far hallway. `NotePlacer.HopsToRooms` counts portal hops. `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=gap` frames gaps for screenshots. The smoke test crosses a real gap and falls in.

- Dynamic UI scaling (`UiScaling`): the HUD, menus and story panels are laid out for 1280 by 720 and scale with the window, so they keep their share of the screen on a small laptop, a 4K or a retina display. Settings has an Interface size slider (60 to 160 percent) and a switch to turn the automatic sizing off. Scales move in whole steps of 0.05 so dragging the window edge does not shimmer text, and the portal views still render at the window's real pixels.
- A hidden figure: the Wulver, who is named Eòghann (the Gaelic form of the author's name, and no one else in the game bears it). In Shetland folklore he is a gentle wolf-headed man who fishes from a rock and leaves his catch for the poor; here he stands on a hill with a branch of yew, 45 to 110 blocks from the dungeon entrance you spawn beside, on the highest level, dry, open ground in that band (`WulverPlacer`, a pure function of the seed). Press Interact within about 3 blocks and he speaks under his name; the first time he also gives a gift: a full heal, your hard damage cleansed and an Open guard perk for 40 seconds, and a fish is left at his feet. Later presses give him other lines, in a seeded order that never repeats until all have been heard. His voice (`WulverVoice`, nine lines) and meeting (`WulverEncounter`) are in Core with tests; `WulverView` draws him (a new low-poly `wulver` model of about 440 triangles with a rock, a fish and a yew branch with red arils), breathing, turning his head to you and swaying the branch, with a faint warm light; `WulverDirector` connects Interact to him. A new soft `WulverGreeting` sound. `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=wulver`, `wulver_wide` and `wulver_talk` place the player beside him for screenshots. A test keeps his name out of every other string in Core and the game scripts.
- `StoryUi.ShowSpeech` shows a speaker's name above a line, in whichever of the game's fonts draws every letter of it.

A new low-poly `wulver` model (440 triangles, with a rock, a fish and a yew branch with red arils), `WulverView` idle animation and a warm light, a soft `WulverGreeting` sound, and `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=wulver`, `wulver_wide`, `wulver_talk` and `wulver_maker`. - The choice panel: `StoryUi.ShowDialogue` shows a speaker's name above their line and a button for each reply, with keyboard and gamepad focus, closing with Interact or the menu button. `StoryUi.ShowSpeech` shows a speaker's name above a caption, in whichever of the game's fonts draws every letter of it.
- Relationship system, phase 1 of the move toward an RPG (`docs/relations.md`): a faction graph (the Hollow, the Folk, wanderers and vermin) with a stance for every pair and a ripple table, so killing a Husk warms the Folk a little and killing a brownie turns them against you. Your standing with each faction runs -100 to 100 in seven tiers (Hated to Revered) with hysteresis, drifts back toward neutral, and is saved per seed in `relations.json` in the data folder (`HOLLOWFALL_RELATIONS` overrides the path).
- Every creature has a disposition (Passive, Skittish, Neutral, Territorial, Curious, Aggressive, Allied) worked out from its faction's regard for you, its species, a seeded personality (bravery, grudge, curiosity) and what it remembers: being hurt, seeing kin hurt (witnesses within 14 blocks with a clear view, with an echo to those who hear the alarm), gifts. Provoked creatures warn before they attack, calm down as they forget, and allies never turn on you.
- `CombatSim.EnableRelations()` switches it on: new `ReputationChanged`, `EntityProvoked`, `EntityCalmed`, `WitnessAlerted`, `EntityAllied`, `BoonGiven`, `OmenSensed` and `OmenFulfilled` events. The Hollow's enemies are unaffected (they chase exactly as before) and creatures are never part of an encounter, so a room with only creatures does not lock and clearing counts only the monsters.
- Five new creatures, appended as `SpawnKind.Creature` after every existing spawn (existing spawns do not move): Brownie, Selkie, Kelpie, Bean Nighe and Hare (see `docs/relations.md`).
- The creatures are drawn and heard: five rigged low-poly models and a shallow pool for the round cistern rooms (appended to `ModelCatalog.All`, each under the usual 260-triangle budget), animated in the game (scurrying brownies, a kelpie that rears before it lunges, a Bean Nighe wringing her cloth, a hopping hare), their own shard colours when they break, and ten new synthesised sounds (appended to `SoundId`): a hiss, a selkie's cry, a kelpie's whinny, a wail, a hare's scream, an omen toll, standing up and down, a boon chime and an ally joining.
- The game plays with the relationship system on. The pause menu has a **Standing** screen (each faction, its tier, a bar and the latest changes), a toast announces a change of tier (and an omen, and a brownie's boon), and a faint pip over a creature you have noticed shows how to read it (green friendly, yellow wary, red hostile, blue allied). Your standing is saved per seed in `relations.json` in the data folder (`HOLLOWFALL_RELATIONS` overrides the path). `StandingBoard` in Core decides the rows, text and pip colours.
- `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=folk` lines the Folk up in the first room, for screenshots; `HOLLOWFALL_MENU=standing` opens the Standing screen.

### Changed

- The README is rewritten: the merge damage is gone (a duplicated controls row and two tangled Settings paragraphs), the status, controls, save folder, features, environment variables and documentation index are up to date, and the hidden things stay spoiler-free.
- The Gàidhlig and Irish docs gained an independent re-check against primary sources (Firefox's Gaelic file, the Ó Dónaill dictionary, Wiktionary): nothing needed fixing, and the remaining doubts (the gender of *loch* and *tobar*, *Air ais* as a button, the register of the greetings) are written down for a speaker.
- Replays are recorded under `RunReplay.SimVersion` 4 (the game now simulates with the relationship system on); older ghosts are unverifiable, and the replay golden checksum in `ReplayTests` changed with the version byte.
- Chests hold real loot, rolled from the seed (`LootTable`): the same dungeon always holds the same loot. Ordinary chests are common; locked chests (treasure and boss rooms) roll a better table with stronger, longer effects. Outcomes are a vial of blood (health), a cleansing drop (clears hard damage), a style shard (a burst of style), Bloodlust (hits heal more for a while), Open guard (a longer parry window for a while) and, from ordinary chests only, a spare key.
- Opening a chest releases a floating pickup (five new low-poly models: vial, drop, star, fang and shield) and the banner names the loot. `ChestOpened` now carries the `Loot` and what it did.
- `PlayerVitals.Cleanse` and `HitHealScale`, `Interactables.Perks`, and `CombatSim.ParryWindowLeft`: small hooks the perks use. The props showcase page shows the new pickups.

- The gift has a moment: a short synthesised chime (`SoundId.WulverGift`, appended at the end of the list), a warm vignette at the screen's edge that swells and fades (`StoryUi.ShowGiftGlow`), a pulse of warm light round him, and the fish pops in at his feet with a small hop instead of appearing. They run on real time, since the game is paused behind the panel. `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=wulver_gift` shows it.
- Smoke checks in the real scene for the maker's routes: seed `ewan` (claim the hill, the maker's lines show, `makerKnown` is saved, he greets the maker by name on return), an ordinary seed (the claim earns the kind reply and the name never shows) and the key route (a key on the last floor, shown to him, `makerKey` and `makerKnown` saved), and one for the conversation camera.
### Changed

- The HUD's key count, the journal's note count and the encounter banner no longer change their wording with the number ("Keys 2", "Notes read: 2", "Enemies 2: kill them to open the doors"), so no language needs plural forms.
### Changed

- Dungeon generation changed: every seed's floors now have gap hallways and pits, so floor layouts, entrance chunks and spawns differ. `RunReplay.SimVersion` is 4, so older ghosts are unverifiable. Floor 0 is about half gaps (the fewest the every-room-has-a-gap rule allows).

### Fixed

- The conversation panel no longer covers the Wulver. `ConversationCamera` eases the view back and turns it so his whole figure sits in the clear screen above the panel, a little to one side, at any window size and for any amount of text (it reads where the panel's top edge is), and eases home when the panel closes. The panel is also lower and tighter.
- The Wulver is easier to find. `WulverPlacer` now scores columns 45 to 140 blocks from the entrance by prominence (height above the mean of the land 8 to 16 blocks around, new `WulverSpot.Prominence`) as well as height, so a knoll beats a higher plateau; rejects spots with trees or cactus within 2 blocks; and, among the best few that hold a level 3 by 3 patch, prefers an open sky (fewer leaf, log and cactus blocks within 7) and a clear line from 14, 28 and 42 blocks back toward the entrance to his head. It remains a pure function of the seed. Nothing is removed from the world: on a very flat seed such as `voxel` the rise is still gentle (about a block above its surroundings), but he now stands against the horizon instead of a wall of trees.
- Walking up a hill no longer jerks the camera. Stepping up a ledge still moves the body at once, but the eye now glides up to the new height (`StepSmoothing`), over a fraction of a second.
- Two portals on the floor no longer trap you. A slow body came out of a portal with its feet still inside the one-block pit behind the exit, could not walk sideways, and fell back in forever. The body is now moved out along the exit's normal until it is fully clear (half its height for floors and ceilings, half its width for walls), and the minimum exit speed is 3 blocks per second. A floor pair is still a loop if you stand still, but steering gets you out, and closing the pair with the Close portals action (C) always does.
- Chest lids now swing open in the game (the props view never registered its chests, so the lid animation never ran).
- Every locked chest can be opened: each floor now hides one key per locked chest on it, in a standard or trap room (this changes the spawns of existing seeds), and replays are recorded under `RunReplay.SimVersion` 3, so older ghosts are unverifiable.
- A spike trap no longer hurts a player on the floor below, and only counts as having struck when it did damage (a parry's invulnerability no longer spares you for the whole strike).
- A respawn no longer leaves the floor's style baseline stale (which could give a negative style grade) or keeps kills from rooms that must be fought again (the HUD could show 12/10).
- Portal views are removed when a respawn clears the portals.
- Rebinding onto a stick axis used by a Look action is refused with a message instead of silently unbinding Look; when another action is left unbound the message says so.
- Settings: Reset rebuilds the sliders so they show the defaults and keeps the chosen seed.
- A damaged save with a repeated property name (settings, records, story progress) falls back to defaults instead of throwing.
- Reloading the scene no longer leaves a menu-sound handler pointing at a freed audio player.
- Personal-best eviction is by recency: improving a record keeps its seed.

### Changed

- All saved data now lives in one folder, `~/.ewanc26/hollowfall` (`DataHome`; `HOLLOWFALL_HOME` moves it): settings, personal bests, replays, story progress and, later, relationships. A first run copies files an earlier version saved in Godot's user folder and leaves the old ones in place.
- Scottish flavour: dungeon seed names now include Scots words (`dreich-howe-318`, `haar-bothy-042`), and a few lore notes and floor cards nod to the kelpie, the bean nighe, brownies, the Cailleach and the Fortingall Yew. `docs/mythology.md` records the sources and how sure they are.
- Chests no longer always heal: the flat 30 (60 locked) health is now one possible outcome, at the same amounts for common and rare chests.

- Free-fly moved to G so that F can open chests. Saved bindings keep working.
- Portals have their own identity: teal and violet chamfered double-frame gates with a diagonal shimmer, replacing the blue and orange ovals. The colours, actions (`PortalTeal`, `PortalViolet`), icons and text were renamed to match; saved bindings for the old action names fall back to the defaults.
- The game is now called Hollowfall. Namespaces, projects, the solution, the Godot project and the environment variables (`HOLLOWFALL_SEED`, `HOLLOWFALL_SPAWN_FLOOR`) were renamed from `VoxelGame`.
- Dungeon entrances are only placed where the surface is at or below y=84 (`DungeonMap.MaxEntranceY`), so the entrance spiral never exceeds about 46 blocks. Dungeons are still in about a fifth of regions (17-22% across five seeds).

### Fixed

- Edited chunks (such as a locked door) are no longer unloaded and silently regenerated; `VoxelWorld.Unload` keeps them unless forced.
- Portal cameras use a better near plane at angles, and portals seen inside other portals draw as frames.

### Added

- Meteor (momentum kills, `MeteorTuning`, `MeteorTracker`): for 0.6 seconds after leaving a portal at 18 blocks per second or more, touching an enemy within 1.2 blocks hits it for speed x 2.5 damage (capped at 120) and awards the Meteor style bonus (180 points, doubled after a fall of more than 20 blocks). The impact costs 30% of your speed (the host applies `MeteorImpact.SpeedKept`), never damages you, and a wall ends the window. A 0.4 second cooldown and a 2 second per-enemy lockout stop a looping portal pair from farming one enemy. It has its own sound (`SoundId.Meteor`) and a METEOR banner. `PlayerView` gained optional `Velocity` and `Crossed` fields for it.
- Sever (docs/features.md 2.1): closing a portal on an enemy cuts it in half. Replacing a portal (placing a new one of the same colour) or removing it (the new Close portals action, `C` / Back, or `CombatSim.ClosePortal` for pacts and bosses) hits every enemy overlapping the old 1 x 2 opening plus 0.3 blocks in front for 60 damage. Kills drop no blood and earn the Sever style bonus (250 points, once per enemy); heavy enemies are staggered instead of killed, Wardens are immune and the player is never hurt. New `PortalClosed` and `EnemySevered` events, a cutting-arc and snap-shut effect, shards and a synthesised sound.
- Replays and ghosts (`Hollowfall.Core.Replay`): every run's fixed-step inputs are recorded compactly (per-tick deltas and run-lengths, capped at 20 minutes and 2 MiB) with the seed, site and format and sim versions, and replay deterministically through `CombatSim`; a state hash and `ReplayRunner.Verify` check that a replay really earns the grade it claims. The best run per seed and floor is saved in a `replays` folder beside `records.json` (loaded defensively, at most 64 files), and shown on that seed and floor as a translucent Husk ghost.
- Polish pass. Water is animated inside its own atlas tile (`EffectsWater`, a code-built shader that wraps a drifting, rippling position over the tile and samples with the nearest filter, so snow and gravel never scroll across a lake). Killed enemies burst into chunky shards in their own palette (`Gibs`, `ShardField`: pooled, deterministic, bouncing off the floor and walls, no blood) and every hit throws sparks from where it landed (gold for head shots). Footsteps are chosen by the block underfoot and speed (`Footsteps`, `Stride`: nine synthesised step sounds, a stride timer, a heavier landing sound, a splash when wading) and played by `AudioDirector` from the physics step, so they stop under the pause menu. Faint dust motes drift in the dungeons (`EffectsDust`). `HOLLOWFALL_SHOWCASE=effects` shows all of it; `HOLLOWFALL_DUST=0` turns the motes off for comparison shots.
- Dungeon depth themes (`DungeonThemes`, `TileSelector`): floor 0 is plain brick, floor 1 is damp and mossy, and floor 2 and deeper are ashen, scorched and ember-lit. The theme is a pure function of height (floors sit 8 blocks apart), so the mesher needs no site; only dungeon blocks are themed and the surface plaza stays plain. Each theme has its own brick, flagstone, pillar and step tiles and a palette tint, plus sparse hash-chosen decals: cracks, cobwebs under the ceiling, bones, moss patches and hanging vines, puddles, scorch marks, glowing ember cracks and coals. A secret door is chosen exactly as its wall is, in every theme. The atlas grew from 25 to 47 tiles (6 rows, 128 pixels high); existing tiles keep their places.
- Natural surface variants chosen by neighbours, with no new blocks and no change to generated worlds: mossy stone beside grass, leaves, logs or water, packed snow on the sides of drifts, and dry cracked earth where soil is open to the air.
- A richer sky built in code (`SkyBuilder`, a sky shader kept as a string): a zenith to horizon gradient, a sun disc with a glow at the angle of the directional light, and two layers of drifting clouds. The fog and ambient light ease toward the theme of the floor you are on: cold grey, green damp, then a dull ember glow.
- A story (`Hollowfall.Core.Story`, designed in `docs/story.md`): a Hollow falling in on itself, seven Wardens who sealed it with the fold-craft, and a Delver following their red cord down to the last Warden, the boss. About sixty authored fragments (twenty-three lore notes in threads by depth band, floor title cards with epigraphs, an opening, floor-complete lines per grade, boss intros, death lines and an ending) are chosen by `StoryPlan` as a pure function of the seed and floor count: each seed names its dungeon and its last Warden, no note repeats in a run, every floor gets its own title, and the notes on a floor are one voice in reading order.
- Lore notes (`NotePlacer`): one to three low-poly scrolls lie against the walls of each floor, in rooms reachable on foot without a secret door (never the entrance, boss or trap rooms, nor near stairs, doors, spawns or chests). Walk up and press Open to read one in a panel; Open or the menu button closes it. Read notes go into a Journal on the pause menu and are saved per seed in `story.json` (`HOLLOWFALL_STORY` overrides the path; loading is defensive and bounded).
- The title screen shows the dungeon's name for the seed; the first play of a seed opens with a short crawl; a floor title card fades in on first entering each floor; lines mark a finished floor, the boss waking and death; an ending card follows the last Warden.
- A scroll model (`ModelCatalog.Scroll`), two sounds (`NoteRead`, `TitleCard`), and story demos for screenshots (`HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=notes|ending|captions`, `HOLLOWFALL_MENU=journal`).

- Smoke checks for the volume sliders, the minimap, opening a chest, a spike trap's damage, personal bests and changing the seed, all driven through the real scene; the smoke test now points `HOLLOWFALL_RECORDS` at a temporary file as well as `HOLLOWFALL_SETTINGS`. `KnownBugTests` holds skipped tests that document bugs found in review.
- Environment textures, drawn by code (`BlockTextures`): a 16 by 16 pixel tile for every surface (stone, dirt, grass top and side, sand, sandstone, snow, water, gravel pebbles, bark and log rings, leaves, cactus ribs, ore flecks, grass blades, flowers, dungeon bricks, flagstones, fluted pillars, steps, iron gates and doors), packed into one atlas. Tiles are light-and-shade patterns tinted by each block's palette colour and ambient occlusion, wrap at their edges, and are flipped by block position to hide repeats. A secret door uses exactly the wall's tile. `docs/texture-atlas.png` is a preview, written by `tools/Hollowfall.AssetGen`.
- A minimap under the floor panel (`FloorMap`): it fills in by line of sight as you explore, secret doors look like wall until found, and the exit stairs show once seen.
- Choose a world: the title screen has a seed box (any text or a number) and a Random button that makes names like `amber-hollow-914`; the seed is saved in settings, and the pause menu has a Main menu button.
- Personal bests (`RunRecords`): each floor's best grade, points and time per seed is saved to `records.json`, and the floor-complete banner says whether it is a new best.
- Chests, keys and spike traps now work (`Interactables`). Walk over a key to take it; press Open (F, or left stick click) beside a chest to open it for health; chests in treasure and boss rooms are locked and spend a key (one per floor). Spike traps warn, then strike on their own beat for 22 damage, and a jump clears them. The floor panel shows keys held, and chests and spikes animate.
- Sound, with no audio files: every effect and the dungeon drone is synthesised in Core (`Synth`), so it is identical everywhere and unit tested (bounded, click-free, deterministic, a seamless loop). Combat events map to sounds in one tested place (`SoundMap`), world sounds play from where they happen, and menus tick and select. Settings gained master, effects and ambience volume.
- Low-poly art for everything that is not a voxel: a code-built model system (`Hollowfall.Core.Models`) with flat-shaded primitives, named pivoted parts with parent links, glow buffers and anchors; models for the three enemies, the enemy orb, the revolver and shotgun, chests, keys, spike traps and torches. See `docs/art-style.md`.
- Enemies are procedurally animated from their state (limb swing, windup poses that flash yellow, stagger), first-person weapon models with kick, muzzle flash and portal-colour gems, and dungeon props including deterministic wall torches (`PropPlacer`) with flickering lights near the player.
- A model showcase scene (`HOLLOWFALL_SHOWCASE=enemies|poses|weapons|props`) and tests for triangle budgets, hit boxes, mirroring and rigging.
- Portal polish: the inside of a portal is now exactly the other camera's image with nothing laid over it (no tint, shimmer or inner lines), the glowing frame sits outside the opening, new portals open with a short grow animation, and the portal camera matches the main view's lens, size and anti-aliasing. The HUD fades out wherever it would cover a portal opening, and damage and parry flashes are edge vignettes that keep the centre clear.
- HUD refinements: a floor panel (floor, kills, time against par), a debug overlay that is off until F3 is pressed, display-font banners, and the wordmark logo on the title screen.
- Assets: Chakra Petch (titles) and Rajdhani (menus and HUD), both SIL OFL, with their licences; a generated vector set (`tools/Hollowfall.AssetGen`) of controller, mouse and keyboard prompts, HUD icons, style-rank emblems, the app icon and the README banner; and `assets/CREDITS.md`. A test fails if the committed files drift from the generator.
- Controller prompts for every face button in three layouts: Xbox (A, B, X, Y), PlayStation (cross, circle, square, triangle) and Nintendo (labels swapped by position), with matching shoulders, triggers and menu buttons. Prompts follow the connected controller, or can be forced in Settings.
- The HUD and menus show glyphs and keycaps for each binding, a rank emblem, a heart for health and the two portal icons on the crosshair.
- Remappable controls for keyboard, mouse and gamepad. Core has `GameAction`, `BindingMap` (one keyboard/mouse and one gamepad binding per action, swap on conflict, Escape reserved for pause, required actions stay bound), clamped `GameSettings` with a defensive, atomically saved JSON store, and the dead-zone and look maths in `InputMath`. In Godot, `InputMapper` turns bindings into InputMap actions (physical keys, so WASD works on AZERTY).
- Gamepad play: left stick moves, right stick looks (radial dead zone, response curve, adjustable speed), triggers fire, shoulders place portals.
- Menus built in code and usable with keyboard, mouse or gamepad: a title screen, a pause menu, a Controls screen with click-to-rebind, and a Settings screen (sensitivity, gamepad look speed, dead zone, field of view, render distance, inverted Y). The game pauses while a menu is open, and hints on the HUD show the buttons for the device you are using.
- HUD polish: damage and parry flashes, hit markers, rank-coloured style meter, health number and an end-of-floor result panel.
- Enemies now use portals as shortcuts to reach you.
- Portals: Q and E place a linked blue and orange pair on flat portalable surfaces. You walk or fall through with your speed conserved (a fall into a floor portal launches you out of a wall), and shots, enemy projectiles and reflected projectiles pass through too. Core has `PortalSystem`, `PortalTransform` and `PortalWalker`; `PlayerBody` gained horizontal momentum and ground friction; the Godot view draws each portal with its own camera and a code-built shader.
- Combat in the game: left and right mouse fire the revolver and shotgun, V parries, doors lock during encounters, and a HUD shows health with hard damage, the style meter, recent bonuses, the floor and the portals. R respawns after dying, and each floor is graded when you go down.
- `HOLLOWFALL_DEMO=portals` runs a scripted portal setup for screenshots.
- Playable world in Godot: chunks stream in around you, are meshed on worker threads, and are drawn with a first-person controller (WASD, jump, mouse look, free-fly) and a HUD. The game spawns at the nearest dungeon entrance; `HOLLOWFALL_SEED` and `HOLLOWFALL_SPAWN_FLOOR` choose the world and a starting dungeon floor.
- `ChunkMesher`: face-culled chunk meshes across chunk borders, with per-vertex ambient occlusion, separate opaque, cutout and water buffers, and an engine-free output format. `VoxelWorld` is a thread-safe chunk cache over the generator.
- `PlayerBody` voxel physics (gravity, jumping, wall sliding, one-block auto-step) and `Navigation` path finding. Tests walk real dungeons from the surface plaza to the boss through real collision.
- The Godot smoke test now boots the real scene and checks that the world streams in and the player lands.
- `.mcp.json` registers the third-party Coding-Solo godot-mcp server (pinned to 0.1.1) so agents can run the game, read its output and edit scenes. `AGENTS.md` records how to use it without breaking the C#-only rule.
- `--dungeon-stats` CLI option reports dungeon density and entrance depth over 256 regions.
- Godot 4.7 (.NET) project shell in `game/`, referencing `Hollowfall.Core`, with a headless smoke test (`run-smoke-test.sh`).
- C#-only language policy for the whole repository, enforced by `LanguagePolicyTests` (no GDScript or GDExtension; scenes may only attach `.cs` scripts) and a pre-commit hook.
- Dungeons: `Hollowfall.Core.Dungeon` builds seeded multi-floor dungeons. Each floor is a BSP layout of rect, L, circular and cavern rooms joined by 3-wide corridors, with doors, open loops and secret doors, and rule-based room roles (entrance, exit, boss, treasure, trap, safe). Floors are stacked 8 blocks apart and joined by spiral stairs; floor 0 is entered from a paved plaza on the surface.
- `DungeonMap` places at most one dungeon per 384-block region on dry, solid ground. `TerrainGenerator` stamps it into chunks (pass `enableDungeons: false` to turn this off), so chunks inside a dungeon footprint differ from earlier worlds.
- Dungeon blocks: stone brick, brick floor, door, secret door, pillar and stairs.
- Deterministic spawn data (monsters, boss, loot, keys, traps) scaled by depth and room role.
- `--dungeon` (and `--spawns`) CLI options print a floor plan and statistics for the dungeon nearest the origin.
- Tall grass and flowers on grassy plains and forests (`BlockInfo.IsPlant`; plants are not solid). Worlds generated before this change differ.
- Rivers: noise zero-crossings carve channels down to just below sea level, fading out toward the sea and thinning in mountains. New `River` biome with a gravel bed. Worlds generated before this change differ.
- `--biomes` option on the CLI prints a coarse ASCII biome map.
- Coal, iron and gold ore veins from per-ore 3D noise, each limited to a depth band (`OreSpec`). Worlds generated before this change differ.
- Seeded terrain generator: Perlin/fBm heightmap, six biomes, caves, trees and cacti over 16×128×16 chunks.
- `Hollowfall.Cli`, a seed inspector that prints a top-down map, block counts and biome statistics.
- Determinism tests covering seed stability, generation order and chunk borders.
